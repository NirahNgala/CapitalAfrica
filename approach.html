Our Investment Approach

A staged process that reduces risk before significant capital is committed.

Project Development Process

Confirm land and partnership → test ecological and commercial feasibility → validate the operating model → structure finance → remain involved through implementation and long-term management.

Land & Partnership → Feasibility → Development Agreement → Pilot & Validation → Investment → Implementation → Monitoring → Revenue & Impact
1

Origination

We identify suitable land, landowners, ecological conditions, species, carbon opportunities, local stakeholders, markets and potential investment partners.

2

Screening

We assess whether the opportunity has credible land rights, environmental suitability, access, water, scale, partner alignment and revenue potential.

3

Feasibility and Structuring

Land and tenure review, baseline ecological assessment, forestry or agroforestry design, carbon pre-feasibility, financial modelling, safeguards, benefit-sharing design and development-agreement preparation.

4

Pilot and Validation

Where required, we establish trials or pilot areas to validate species performance, propagation, planting systems, survival, operating costs, community engagement and monitoring protocols.

5

Investment Preparation

Technical documentation, commercial models, governance arrangements, carbon design, implementation budgets and staged capital requirements.

6

Implementation

Capital Africa acts as developer, local operating partner, joint-venture partner or land-development manager, coordinating nursery production, planting, infrastructure, staffing and stakeholder implementation.

7

Monitoring and Value Realisation

We monitor forestry growth, carbon, biodiversity, farm production and social outcomes while developing markets for carbon credits, timber, moringa, honey and other outputs.

Investment Model

Capital Africa generally does not seek to own all project land. We prefer long-term development structures in which institutional landowners, private landowners or organised farmers retain their land while Capital Africa brings project development, investment mobilisation, technical systems and long-term management.

The commercial structure may include a contractual joint venture, long-term development agreement, revenue-sharing agreement, outgrower contract or carbon participation agreement. Capital may be assembled from development grants, carbon pre-finance, patient equity, long-term debt, equipment finance, strategic offtake and reinvested operating revenue.
