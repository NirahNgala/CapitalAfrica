The proof point

Not a promise.
A practiced system.

Capital Africa began developing Koromi Farm in 2019 as a practical response to a central question: can commercially relevant forestry, biodiversity conservation and ecological restoration be combined under dryland conditions?

Koromi is where we learn in the conditions that matter: variable rainfall, demanding soils, local seed systems and the long horizon of land stewardship. The site was developed progressively—not as a showcase plantation. Every planting cycle gives better answers on species, water, nursery quality, spacing, pruning, survival and operational management before we scale them beyond the farm.

What Koromi has taught us

The work behind the outcome.

Practical lessons from the field that cannot be obtained from feasibility studies alone. They guide every larger decision Capital Africa makes.

01

Choose for the site.

Species selection follows the reality of soil, rainfall and water access—not a template. A species that performs well in one dryland environment may fail where conditions differ.

02

Planting density shapes form and growth.

Higher initial density can improve height growth and stem straightness while creating opportunities for early pole thinnings.

03

Make water a system.

Storage, pumping, planting season and targeted field distribution must operate as one. Reservoirs and distribution are managed together, not in isolation.

04

Build quality before scale.

Strong nurseries, root structure, hardening, seed source, capable teams and reliable field operations are the foundation of credible growth.

05

Early silviculture is essential.

Pruning, thinning, protection and replacement cannot be postponed without affecting timber quality and future value.

06

Protect the whole landscape.

Conservation and commercial forestry work best as connected parts of the same plan—productive blocks, natural vegetation, enrichment planting and biodiversity areas together.

07

Operational capacity precedes scale.

People, machinery, seed systems, logistics, monitoring and decision-making structures must be in place before large planting targets become credible.

For partners & investors

Why Koromi matters.

Koromi gives Capital Africa practical knowledge that cannot be obtained from feasibility studies alone. It allows the company to test forestry systems, water solutions, species combinations, restoration methods and monitoring approaches before applying them at larger scale.

It demonstrates understanding of the realities of dryland land development: variable rainfall, seedling losses, fire risk, nursery timing, biodiversity trade-offs, maintenance requirements and the need for long-term local management.
