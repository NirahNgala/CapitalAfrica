Partnerships

Capital Africa’s role is to combine the contributions of landowners, investors, carbon specialists, technical institutions, government agencies and communities into projects with clear responsibilities and long-term commercial discipline.

Who We Partner With

Each partner type brings a distinct contribution. We structure clear roles, responsibilities and benefit-sharing from the outset.

Institutional Landowners

Universities, public institutions, development authorities, churches, trusts and private estates that control suitable land but may lack finance or operating capacity.

  • Land-use concept and feasibility development
  • Long-term development and revenue-sharing agreements
  • Investment mobilisation
  • Project implementation and management
  • Improvement of the productive and environmental value of the land

Carbon Developers & Credit Buyers

Capital Africa provides the local origination, operating and land-partnership platform needed to develop credible projects.

  • Access to land and counterparties
  • Field implementation and monitoring
  • Nursery and planting capacity
  • Stakeholder engagement
  • Long-term local management
  • Transparent benefit-sharing administration

Impact & Natural Capital Investors

A pipeline of projects that combine commercial outputs with measurable environmental and social outcomes.

  • Investment-ready project development
  • Local implementation platform
  • Technical and commercial structuring
  • Governance and reporting
  • Long-term asset management

Universities & Research Institutions

Research partnerships strengthen species selection, silviculture, carbon accounting, biodiversity monitoring and land-management innovation.

  • Applied field trials
  • Student and researcher engagement
  • Monitoring and data systems
  • Joint funding proposals
  • Demonstration and training sites

Government Agencies & Public Authorities

Public-sector partners can provide land, policy alignment, technical mandates, protection support and links with communities.

  • Landscape restoration programmes
  • Sustainable forestry partnerships
  • Public-private development structures
  • Technical demonstrations
  • Local economic development

Communities & Outgrowers

Communities and landowners are long-term project participants rather than short-term labour sources.

  • Transparent agreements
  • Employment and contracting
  • Outgrower production
  • Carbon and produce benefit sharing
  • Training and local enterprise opportunities
